Website audit: staging.southwindmarketing.com

Assessment site for Southwind Marketing · Read-only review · WordPress on Cloudways / nginx

1. Executive summary

This is a professional staging site, not a rough draft. The positioning is specific, the templates share one visual system, and a lot of the usual QA work is already done: one H1 per page, labeled forms, a real 404, keyboard focus, and a mobile menu with usable hit areas. I would be comfortable showing this to a client as a preview.

I would not flip it to the public production domain yet. Three things should be fixed first. The proof-stat component breaks its own numbers when the figure is a phrase, and I confirmed that at desktop width, not only on a phone. The contact form asks a visitor to scan 15 services before they can say what they need. The SEO template is missing a canonical on every page I checked, and the homepage has no share image.

Launch recommendation: hold the public launch for the stat component, the contact form, and the production metadata switch. The current noindex, nofollow is the correct staging setting. I am not counting it as a defect.

Highest-priority recommendations

  • Rebuild stat figures so the number stays on one line. Split words like “From” and “weeks” into the caption.
  • Shorten /contact/ to name, email, organization type, one need, a message, and the SMS consent.
  • Before production: add self-referencing canonicals, a real homepage og:image, and turn indexing on only at launch. Keep noindex on this staging host.

2. What’s working

These should stay.

  • The offer is concrete. The homepage H1 is about leads, calls, and revenue. Service pages lead with a price, a timeline, or a client result instead of a slogan. The City of Ulysses and A&K case studies name the client and the number.
  • One visual system. Black #080A0C, paper white, one blue #027AB9, one green #43AF48, Instrument Sans. Interior pages feel like the same site as the homepage.
  • Navigation is built for both pointer and keyboard. Desktop items are a link plus a separate menu button with aria-expanded. The mobile menu is a details element. Targets are about 44–52px. Escape closes an open menu.
  • The logo strip is done correctly. It pauses on hover, it stops for prefers-reduced-motion, and the duplicated set of logos is aria-hidden. Logo images use empty alt because the organization name is real text next to the image.
  • Forms are real forms. Labels are tied to inputs, email uses autocomplete="email", the honeypot is display: none, and the SMS line says consent is not required to buy. Phone numbers are tel: links. Email is mailto:.
  • Fewer stats already reflow. Strips with two or three figures switch to two or three columns, so Locations and Restaurants do not leave an empty fourth column.
  • Staging is marked as staging. Every sampled page sends noindex, nofollow. A missing URL returns HTTP 404 and the title “Page Not Found - Southwind Marketing.”
  • Focus is visible. :focus-visible uses a 3px outline in #3BA3DB, which is about 7:1 on the dark background.

3. Design and user experience

Overall the site looks like a finished agency site: strong type, generous space, and photography that supports the rural and civic story. The weakness is in a few components where a long string meets a large type size, and in pages that repeat the same action under a different name.

Stat figures wrap at desktop

Where: /services/website-design/, the “Results in numbers” strip. Same component on other templates.

What I measured: at a 1440px viewport the figures are 70px type in a 313px column. “Under 2s” stays on one line. “From $5,500”, “8 to 14 weeks”, and “16 to 24 weeks” wrap to two lines. The same wrap is there at 1100px and at 830px. Homepage figures (“110+”, “100,000+”, “$2M+”, “50 states”) stay on one line at 1440px, so this is the phrase-length figures, not the whole homepage.

Why it matters: this strip is the proof under the headline. When three of the four numbers break and one does not, the row looks unfinished. A city manager scanning “16 to 24 weeks” should read one figure, not a wrapped sentence in 70px type.

Correction: put only the number in the large type. Move the qualifier into the caption. For example the second stat becomes $5,500 with the caption “Starting price for a rural small business site, fixed scope.” Do the same for “8–14 weeks” and “16–24 weeks.” Check the same pattern on /solutions/ (“From $129”, “48 hours”, “1 invoice”) and /locations/oklahoma/ (“About 70%”).

Contact page makes people scroll to a long form

Where: /contact/

The first screen is a headline and a “Send us a message” button that jumps to the form. The useful facts (Yukon, the phone number, “within one business day”) sit between the hero and the form. The form then asks for 15 services as individual checkboxes, plus “Other service”, before the required “How can we help?” field. Each checkbox row is styled at a 60px minimum height, so the list is most of a phone screen by itself.

Correction: put the form on the first screen, beside the reply-time and phone. Replace the 15 checkboxes with one organization select and one “what do you need” select. The redesign sample covers this layout.

The same action has too many names

I found four promises for what is really two destinations.

Correction: use “Get a Free Consultation” for /contact/ and “Free Website Audit” for /audit/ everywhere. Use “Get a quote” only next to a price, and send that one to /get-a-quote/.

Iowa page promises a local strategist the copy does not support

Where: /locations/iowa/

The hero button says “Talk to an Iowa strategist.” Further down, the map section says “We have no named Iowa client yet” and points at Kansas work. The honest sentence is the right one. The button overclaims.

Correction: change the button to “Talk with Southwind about Iowa” and keep the Kansas proof labeled as Kansas.

Other design notes

  • There is no breadcrumb on deep pages. On Website Design I had the menu and the logo, and no “Home / Services / Website Design” trail. Add one under the header on service, industry, location, case study, and blog templates.
  • The active item is not marked. On Website Design, nothing in the menu exposes aria-current="page".
  • Team portraits in team.css stay grayscale until hover. A phone has no hover, so they stay gray. Show them in color, and keep a hover treatment if you want one on desktop.
  • Below 1024px the header “Get in touch” link is hidden and the Menu button takes over. That is fine, because the hero still has the two buttons. I would not add a second sticky bar.

5. Technical and SEO review

The staging host is intentionally out of the index. robots meta is noindex, nofollow on every page I saved. That is correct. robots.txt disallows /wp-admin/ except admin-ajax, and it publishes a sitemap. Crawlers can still fetch URLs, and the meta tag tells them not to index. Leave that as it is until launch day.

Titles, descriptions, canonicals, share cards

Titles and descriptions are written by a person and are mostly the right length. Examples: homepage title 51 characters, description 144. Website Design title 53, description 146. A few sit outside the range I would ship:

  • Blog post title “Church Marketing Strategies for Small Teams: Follow Up Within 72 Hours” is 70 characters. Trim the front so the useful words survive in Google.
  • “Portfolio | Southwind Marketing” is 31 characters and says less than the rest of the system. Use the H1 idea: websites, brands, and programs with a result.
  • No page I checked has a rel=canonical. The homepage also has no og:image. I confirmed both in the live DOM. Website Design does have an og:image, and it is the decorative swirl, swirl-frame.webp, with alt “Website Design.”

Correction, for production only: one self-referencing canonical per URL, a homepage share image of the real hero or the logo on a designed card (1200×630), and page-specific share images where the page has a real photo. Switch noindex off only on the production host.

Headings, links, URLs

  • Sampled pages have one H1. The website design outline steps H1 → H2 → H3 without skipping a level. Footer column titles are also H2s, which I cover under accessibility.
  • URLs are short, lowercase, and hyphenated (/services/website-design/, /case-studies/city-of-ulysses-civicplus-replacement/).
  • Internal links in the header, footer, and the sections I opened point at real pages. Case-study stats link to the study they cite.
  • There is no breadcrumb trail and no HTML sitemap for visitors. The XML sitemap exists for crawlers. A breadcrumb is the visitor-facing fix.

Performance and images

  • The homepage HTML response is 292,558 bytes uncompressed. The theme CSS is inlined in the document, so a visitor downloads that CSS again on the next page. Move the shared CSS to one file with long cache headers. Keep critical hero CSS inline if you need it.
  • Hero and content photos are WebP with srcset. The homepage hero uses fetchpriority="high". That part is in good shape.
  • On the pages I measured in the browser, the homepage transferred about 1.1 MB of subresources after HTML, with the hero WebP as the largest image. That is acceptable. The repeated CSS is the part I would fix.

Configuration I could see without touching the admin

  • The homepage response is served by nginx, cached by Cloudways (Breeze, X-Cache: HIT). It did not include Strict-Transport-Security, Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, or Referrer-Policy. Add those at the server before launch. I am flagging the missing headers, not a vulnerability I tried to use.
  • The generator meta reports WordPress 7.1.3. Remove it.
  • The HTML loads Google Tag Manager GTM-TRTC447K and Meta’s fbevents.js. If this container is the live one, every staging visit lands in production analytics. Point staging at a separate container, or pause the tags, until cutover.

6. Accessibility

The base is ahead of most agency sites I review. These are the gaps I would still file.

ItemEvidenceChange
Logo name announced twice The home link is named “Southwind Marketing, home.” The SVG inside it is role="img" with aria-label="Southwind Marketing". Set aria-hidden="true" on the SVG. The link label is enough. Same pattern in the footer logo.
Footer headings Column labels (Services, Solutions, Industries, Locations, Company, Work & Insights) are H2s, at the same level as the page sections. Change them to paragraphs with the existing footer class. Leave one H2 only if the footer needs a heading, such as “Footer.”
Current page Website Design exposes no aria-current. Add aria-current="page" on the matching header and footer link.
Team photos on touch filter: grayscale(1) until :hover. Default to color. Hover can still zoom or lift the card.
Decorative versus informative images Hero swirls and case-study backgrounds use empty alt, which is right while text on top carries the meaning. Logo alts are empty because the name is beside the image. Content photos I checked, such as “The Southwind team on site with a client,” have a real alt. Keep that rule. When a case-study background is the only description of a screenshot, give that image a short alt and don’t hide it.

Contrast on the tokens I calculated is in range for the pairs that carry body text. Examples: green #43AF48 on #080A0C is about 7.1:1 (the “Get in touch” link). Blue-on-dark #3BA3DB on #080A0C is about 7.0:1. Muted text #5E5E5E on white is about 6.5:1. White on the blue button #027AB9 is about 4.7:1, which clears 4.5:1 for the button label. I would not open a contrast ticket on those tokens.

Keyboard: skip link is present, focus outline is visible, menu buttons are real buttons, and the reduced-motion logo strip is in place. I did not complete a full tab pass of every template.

7. Redesign recommendations

I would change the contact page, because that is where the visit either becomes a lead or dies. The rest of the system is strong enough to leave alone.

What changes

  • Remove the “Send us a message” jump. The form is the page.
  • Desktop is two columns. Left: headline, one-business-day promise, phone, location, and three short steps (we reply, we say what we’d do first, you get a fixed scope). Right: the form.
  • The form keeps the underlined fields and the black page, so it still looks like Southwind. It drops the 15 checkboxes. Organization is a select (business, city or school, chamber or EDO, nonprofit). Need is a select (website, SEO, ads and social, CRM, not sure yet). Then the message, the existing SMS consent, and one blue “Send message” button.
  • Phone stays a tap target at the top of the left column, not a line under the email address.

What this improves: a mayor or a shop owner can see how fast you reply and can finish the form without decoding the service catalog. Sales still learns the organization type and the need. The long catalog remains on /services/, which is the right place for it.

The stat strip is a component fix, not a new layout. I would not redesign the homepage. The headline, the two buttons, and the four short figures are doing their job.

A desktop sample of the contact page is in contact-redesign-sample.html in this folder. Rebuild that frame in Figma and share the view link. Notes for the frame are in section 9.

9. Visual design sample

Page: Contact. Frame: desktop, 1440 wide.

Why this page: the homepage already has a clear headline, two actions, and proof. The contact page spends the first screen on a button that only scrolls, then asks for the full service list. That is the page a redesign actually improves.

Decisions in the sample

  • Same brand colors and the same underlined fields, so development can reuse the current form CSS.
  • Left column carries trust (where you are, how fast you reply, what happens next). Right column is the work.
  • Five fields instead of a checklist. The service catalog stays on the services page.
  • One button, “Send message,” in the existing blue. The phone number is a second, quieter path, not a competing button.
